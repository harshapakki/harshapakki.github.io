02 — Method

How the work actually runs

Four steps, in this order, on every product I have owned.

STEP 01

Sit with the operators

The bottleneck is rarely the thing people name in a meeting. At Cornerstone it turned out to be hand-configured installs, not the product.

STEP 02

Agree the number first

Accept and escalate thresholds come from measurement, not intuition. 85% on a golden set, 90% after adjudication.

STEP 03

Test small, test many

Eight to twelve concurrent pricing and packaging experiments, two week minimum run, scale what clears.

STEP 04

Write it up either way

Two roadmap items retired for missing their metric, with the reasoning documented so the next cycle starts from evidence.

03 — Interactive

Unit economics lab

A credit is only as sound as the cost underneath it. This is the four-tier escalation ladder behind an AI extraction product. Move the escalation rate and watch the blended cost per pass move with it.

Why the ladder exists

Running every document through a premium model costs $99.50 a pass. Running none of them through it is cheap and wrong. The question is which 35% deserve the expensive read.

Routing is decided by deterministic confidence drawn from physical cross-checks — OCR presence, geometry, mass derived from geometry against printed weight, catalogue enums — rather than from what the model says about itself. Anything under 0.80 confidence escalates. Everything else is already right.

Calibrated to two measured points: $99.50 for a full premium pass, $42.60 blended at the 35% escalation cap that shipped.
04 — Conversion

The paywall was in the wrong place

Trial users were being asked to pay before the product had shown them anything. Most of the loss was happening before first value, not at the price.

Signed up for trial100%
Reached first successful extraction41%
Converted to paid11%

What changed

50 starter credits, metered exactly the way paid usage is metered, and the paywall moved to sit after the first successful extraction instead of in front of it.

A trial that does not spend enough credits to reach a real result teaches the user the product is mediocre. The fix was not a discount.

05 — Cases

Four problems worth the detail

Monetization

Pricing the business can change itself

  • An 83-field configuration catalogue and 7 governed constants behind an approval workflow
  • A price or package change ships the same day, with no engineering release
  • Three regions on one platform, per-site configuration instead of forks
  • Three-tier plan structure moved 31% of single-site accounts onto a multi-site tier
AI Product

Extraction at a price per unit

  • 1,800 scanned drawings in 3 days for about $150, against a month and $3-5K manual
  • 97 part families, a 214-page catalogue, four-tier escalation ladder
  • Escalation capped at 35%, accuracy 85% on a golden set and 90% adjudicated
  • Priceable per unit instead of per project
Retention

Cancellation as a research instrument

  • Leavers split three ways: never activated, activated and drifted, hit a cost wall
  • Each group routed to a different save offer, not one blanket discount
  • Voluntary churn down 3.4 points
  • Reactivation keyed to the defect behind each lapse recovered 9% in two quarters
Packaging

From services line item to paid add-on

  • Self-serve BI packaged with entitlements and row-level security
  • Customers answered their own reporting questions instead of buying an engagement
  • 65% faster queries, 45% less memory, prioritized by retention risk
  • High availability behind a 99.99% uptime commitment cleared the largest enterprise objection
